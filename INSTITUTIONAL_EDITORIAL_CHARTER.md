## 1. Purpose and authority

This Charter defines the institutional purpose, editorial duties, decision rights, and minimum conduct required for work published or represented under the FutureWorld Intelligence name. It applies to FWI personnel, contractors, contributors, reviewers, advisers, and partners when acting for FWI.

The Charter is FWI's governing editorial standard. Supporting procedures, validation controls, commercial policies, and publishing workflows must implement it and may set stricter requirements. Applicable law takes precedence where it imposes a higher or conflicting obligation.

## 2. Institutional identity and public-interest mandate

FutureWorld Intelligence is an independent educational and analytical platform. Its public-interest mandate is to help people understand consequential changes in climate, artificial intelligence, geopolitics, energy, natural resources, and future systems through accessible, evidence-led work.

FWI's mission is to make complex global change understandable through disciplined research, clear briefings, visual explanation, and practical learning resources. Its vision is a trusted public knowledge space in which citizens, students, professionals, and decision-makers can examine emerging risks, opportunities, choices, and uncertainty.

FWI does not present itself as a government authority, accredited academic institution, regulated professional adviser, intelligence agency, or certification body. Its work is educational and analytical unless a publication explicitly states otherwise.

## 3. Institutional values

FWI shall apply the following values:

1. **Truth and evidence:** seek accuracy, preserve traceability, and correct material errors.
2. **Independence:** protect editorial judgment from political, financial, personal, donor, sponsor, or client pressure.
3. **Public benefit:** prioritize useful understanding over attention, alarm, or institutional self-promotion.
4. **Human dignity and safety:** consider human rights, privacy, security, and foreseeable harm.
5. **Transparency:** distinguish what is known, inferred, assumed, uncertain, simulated, sponsored, or machine-assisted.
6. **Accountability:** retain named human responsibility for every publication and material decision.
7. **Inclusion and accessibility:** avoid discriminatory framing and make work usable by diverse audiences where reasonably possible.
8. **Environmental responsibility:** consider environmental effects in research, technology, and operational choices.

## 4. Scope and editorial classifications

This Charter covers reports, briefings, articles, dashboards, datasets, maps, graphics, videos, courses, newsletters, social posts, and other public or partner-facing outputs carrying the FWI identity.

Material content must be identifiable by type where confusion is reasonably possible:

- **Fact:** a verifiable statement supported by evidence.
- **Analysis:** an evidence-based interpretation or judgment.
- **Estimate or forecast:** a reasoned assessment about an unknown or future condition, with uncertainty stated.
- **Scenario:** a plausible conditional future, not a prediction.
- **Opinion or commentary:** the attributed view of an author or contributor.
- **Sponsored or partner content:** content supported by an external party, with the relationship disclosed.
- **Correction, update, or withdrawal:** a visible change to a previously published record.

Facts, assumptions, analytical judgments, scenarios, and advocacy must not be presented as interchangeable.

## 5. Editorial independence and conflicts of interest

Editorial conclusions must be determined by evidence and documented analysis. A funder, sponsor, donor, client, partner, political actor, adviser, or platform provider may not purchase a conclusion, suppress a material finding, or require the omission of relevant uncertainty or counterevidence.

FWI shall:

- disclose material funding, sponsorship, partnership, employment, financial, political, or personal interests relevant to a publication;
- require contributors and reviewers to declare relevant conflicts;
- recuse or replace a decision-maker where a conflict could reasonably compromise independent judgment;
- label paid, commissioned, or sponsored work so it cannot be mistaken for independently initiated public-interest research; and
- document material editorial conditions attached to external support.

Commercial activity may fund operations but may not override this Charter. The detailed institutional separation of public-interest research from commercial services is governed by a dedicated policy and operating controls.

## 6. Evidence and source standards

Publications must use evidence proportionate to the significance and risk of their claims. FWI shall:

- make major factual claims traceable to identifiable sources;
- prefer primary, official, or directly observed evidence where appropriate;
- corroborate consequential or disputed claims with independent sources where feasible;
- assess source authority, provenance, date, context, incentives, methodology, and limitations;
- recheck facts that are volatile, time-sensitive, or likely to have changed before publication;
- preserve an evidence register and citation audit for formal reports;
- distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence; and
- never cite an AI system's output as evidence for a factual claim.

Anonymous or confidential sources may be used only when the public value justifies the limitation, identity and access are known to an accountable human within FWI, and disclosure would create a credible risk. The publication must describe the source sufficiently for readers to assess the claim without exposing protected identities.

## 7. Analytical integrity and uncertainty

Analysis must be logically connected to the evidence and must not conceal reasonable alternatives. Material analytical products shall, where relevant:

- state the question, scope, timeframe, definitions, and material assumptions;
- separate evidence from inference and judgment;
- communicate uncertainty and confidence in plain language;
- examine credible alternative explanations and meaningful counterevidence;
- identify data gaps, limitations, and conditions that could change the conclusion;
- distinguish forecast, scenario, target, and aspiration; and
- avoid false precision, false balance, sensationalism, and advocacy disguised as neutral analysis.

FWI may use constructive or solutions-oriented framing, but it shall not force optimistic findings that the evidence does not support.

## 8. Responsible use of artificial intelligence

AI may assist research planning, discovery, summarization, drafting, translation, coding, visualization, quality checks, and validation. AI does not hold editorial authority and may not approve or publish material autonomously.

For material work, FWI shall:

- assign a named human who remains accountable for accuracy, legality, fairness, and release;
- independently verify AI-generated claims, quotations, citations, calculations, and interpretations;
- disclose material AI assistance when it could affect a reasonable reader's assessment of provenance or reliability;
- protect confidential, personal, licensed, and security-sensitive information from inappropriate system exposure;
- assess material outputs for bias, fabrication, misleading synthesis, and unsafe recommendations; and
- prohibit fabricated sources, quotations, credentials, evidence, or data and prohibit synthetic media that deceptively represents real events or people.

AI-generated or substantially AI-modified visuals must be labelled when their synthetic nature is not obvious and is material to interpretation. AI output is never a substitute for accountable human judgment.

## 9. Human rights, safety, privacy, and fairness

FWI shall respect human dignity and consider foreseeable effects on individuals, communities, and vulnerable groups. Content must avoid unlawful discrimination, harassment, defamation, dehumanization, and unnecessary disclosure of personal or security-sensitive information.

High-risk topics—including conflict, health, finance, elections, public safety, critical infrastructure, and allegations of wrongdoing—require heightened verification, careful language, and risk review. Maps, images, or data must not reveal sensitive locations or identities where disclosure could create a credible risk of harm without a compelling public-interest justification.

People or organizations facing serious criticism should be given a fair opportunity to respond where feasible, and their relevant response should be reflected accurately.

## 10. Visual, data, copyright, and accessibility integrity

Charts, maps, images, and multimedia must not distort scale, context, or meaning. Editorial illustrations and simplified maps must not be presented as official geospatial products. Material transformations, simulations, or synthetic elements must be labelled where needed to avoid deception.

FWI shall respect copyright, licences, attribution requirements, privacy, and consent. Plagiarism and unattributed republication are prohibited.

Public work should use clear language and, where reasonably possible, meaningful headings, descriptive links, alternative text, captions or transcripts, readable contrast, mobile-compatible layouts, and explanations of specialist terms. Representation must avoid stereotypes and tokenism.

## 11. Corrections, updates, and withdrawals

FWI welcomes substantiated correction requests at **futureworldintelligence@gmail.com**. Reports of error must be assessed impartially and as promptly as reasonably possible.

- Minor non-substantive edits may be corrected without a notice.
- Material corrections must identify what changed, why, and when.
- Significant new evidence should be recorded as an update rather than silently replacing the earlier state.
- Content that is materially false, unsafe, unlawful, or no longer defensible must be suspended, withdrawn, or clearly retracted while preserving an appropriate audit record.

No person may retaliate against a contributor, reviewer, source, or reader for raising a good-faith accuracy or integrity concern.

## 12. Complaints, feedback, and remedy

Editorial complaints may be submitted through the public contact channel. FWI shall record material complaints, acknowledge them, examine the relevant evidence and conflicts, and communicate the outcome. A material complaint should be reviewed by someone other than the sole author where practicable.

Where FWI's work has caused or contributed to a substantiated adverse effect, it shall take proportionate corrective action. Possible remedies include correction, clarification, apology, removal of sensitive material, withdrawal, process improvement, or referral to an appropriate authority.

## 13. Roles and decision rights

| Role | Minimum responsibility |
|---|---|
| Institutional Lead | Adopts governance, protects independence, appoints decision authorities, and addresses systemic breaches. The role may not dictate a finding contrary to the evidence. |
| Author or Producer | Creates the work, maintains its sources, declares conflicts, and responds to review. |
| Editorial Reviewer | Assesses clarity, fairness, classification, public value, legal and ethical concerns, and consistency with this Charter. |
| Validation Reviewer | Checks claims, citations, evidence quality, uncertainty, calculations, and required validation records. |
| Authorized Human Approver | Makes and records the final publish, hold, return, or withdraw decision. |
| Governance Administrator | Maintains templates, permissions, version history, gate configuration, and auditable records. |

Individuals may hold more than one role when capacity is limited, but the same person may not act as sole author, sole validator, and sole approver of a high-risk publication. An external qualified reviewer may satisfy the independence requirement. Conflicts and any reduced separation of duties must be disclosed in the approval record.

## 14. Publication and approval standard

Formal reports and other designated material outputs may be released only when:

1. required intake, evidence, citation, fact-check, validation, and approval records are complete;
2. identified high-risk claims and conflicts have been resolved or expressly documented;
3. the responsible human approver records an unambiguous approval;
4. applicable automated governance checks pass; and
5. the public output includes the disclosures, limitations, and correction route required by this Charter.

“Conditional pass,” unresolved hard-block findings, missing evidence, or an AI-generated approval are not publication approval. Urgency may accelerate review but does not waive the standard. When requirements cannot be met, the decision is to hold, revise, narrow, label as preliminary where appropriate, or withdraw.

## 15. Funding, partnerships, and external influence

FWI shall disclose material external support and retain contractual control over factual accuracy, methods, conclusions, corrections, and withdrawal. Partners may review content for factual errors, confidentiality obligations, and safety concerns but may not convert review into undisclosed editorial control.

Donor, sponsor, or client access does not create preferential influence over independently initiated public-interest research. Audience data, confidential information, and editorial records must not be reused for commercial purposes without an appropriate lawful basis and disclosed governance.

## 16. Enforcement and non-compliance

Any contributor may pause a publication and escalate a credible Charter concern. Material breaches may result in additional review, correction, withdrawal, restricted access, recusal, termination of a contribution or partnership, and changes to controls or training.

Breaches affecting a publication decision must be recorded in its audit trail. Deliberate fabrication, plagiarism, concealed material conflicts, deceptive synthetic media, retaliation, or unauthorized publication are serious violations.

## 17. Transparency, amendment, and review

The current Charter shall remain publicly accessible. Material amendments require versioning, an effective date, institutional approval, and a summary of the change. FWI will review this Charter at least annually and after a serious incident, major operating-model change, or material change in applicable law or technology.

Supporting procedures and templates must be checked for consistency whenever this Charter changes.

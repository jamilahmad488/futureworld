# FutureWorld Intelligence Public-Interest Research and Commercial Services Separation Policy

| Document control | Standard |
|---|---|
| Organization | FutureWorld Intelligence (FWI) |
| Status | Adopted institutional policy |
| Version | 1.0 |
| Effective date | 13 July 2026 |
| Owner | FWI Institutional Lead |
| Governing authority | [Institutional and Editorial Charter](INSTITUTIONAL_EDITORIAL_CHARTER.md) |
| Review cycle | At least annually and after any material funding, service, or operating-model change |
| Public record | `/pages/research-commercial-independence/` |

## 1. Purpose and authority

This Policy prevents commercial relationships from being mistaken for, influencing, or receiving undisclosed preference within FutureWorld Intelligence's public-interest research. It establishes mandatory classification, editorial, financial, contractual, information, branding, and approval boundaries for every FWI workstream.

The Policy applies before FWI accepts funding, begins work, grants access, makes a public claim, or uses the FWI identity in connection with an external party. It applies to personnel, contractors, contributors, advisers, reviewers, sponsors, partners, and clients acting with or for FWI.

This is an operational separation policy. It does not claim that FWI has separate legal entities, charitable or tax-exempt status, regulated advisory status, audited segment accounts, or certification. Any future legal or accounting structure must be described accurately and must preserve or strengthen these controls.

## 2. Non-negotiable principle

Commercial revenue may support FWI's operations, but it may not purchase a conclusion, suppress a material finding, weaken an evidence standard, bypass a correction, select a validator for the purpose of obtaining approval, or cause commercial work to be presented as independently initiated public-interest research.

No contract, payment, relationship, deadline, or confidentiality request overrides the Institutional and Editorial Charter.

## 3. Mandatory workstream classifications

Every workstream must receive one—and only one—of the following classifications at intake:

| Classification | Primary purpose | External party's permitted role | Release and access | Required public label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PI — Public-interest research** | Independently initiated knowledge for broad public benefit | May provide evidence or comment; has no funding-based control | Public release is the default; equal public access after any short administrative embargo | `FWI Public-Interest Research` |
| **PS — Partner-supported public-interest research** | Public-interest work supported by a grant, sponsor, donor, or partner while FWI retains editorial control | May fund, suggest questions, provide data, and review factual or confidentiality issues; may not control conclusions or final approval | Public release is required unless a documented safety or rights exception applies | `Partner-Supported Public-Interest Research` plus named support disclosure |
| **CS — Commercial services** | A paid service, commissioned deliverable, licence, training, production, custom analysis, or other client-directed benefit | May define a lawful brief, delivery format, confidentiality, and acceptance criteria; may not require false or misleading findings | May be private or published under the contract; any public version must be unmistakably commercial or commissioned | `Commercial Service` or `Commissioned Work` plus client and relationship disclosure |

If a workstream does not fit one classification, it must be divided into separate work packages with separate records, permissions, budgets, deliverables, and approvals. “Hybrid” is not an approved classification.

## 4. Classification decision rule

The classification must reflect substance, not terminology. Calling a payment a grant, partnership, contribution, sponsorship, or expense reimbursement does not make the work public-interest.

A workstream is commercial when an external party receives a material private benefit such as a client-specific deliverable, exclusive analysis, confidential advice, customized production, preferred access, licensing rights, or control over scope that is inconsistent with independent public release.

A funded workstream may be partner-supported public-interest research only when all of the following are true:

1. FWI retains control of methods, evidence assessment, conclusions, validation, corrections, and final release.
2. The support and the external party's role will be disclosed clearly.
3. Public release is a binding project objective.
4. The external party cannot suppress material findings or require a favorable conclusion.
5. Any pre-publication access is limited to fact, safety, legal, privacy, or confidentiality review and is documented.

Uncertainty must be resolved before work begins. The Institutional Lead records the classification using the [Workstream Classification Record](governance/workstream-classification-template.yml). Misclassification is a governance incident, not a branding error.

## 5. Editorial firewall

### 5.1 Public-interest work

For PI and PS work, the editorial authority—not a commercial lead, funder, sponsor, donor, or partner—controls the research question as finally framed, methods, source selection, findings, limitations, title, publication timing, correction, update, and withdrawal.

An external party may identify factual errors, confidential information, legal concerns, or credible safety risks. FWI must evaluate those comments independently and preserve a record of material requested and accepted changes.

### 5.2 Commercial work

For CS work, a client may define the brief, intended users, delivery format, timetable, confidentiality, and lawful acceptance criteria. FWI nevertheless retains the right and duty to refuse fabrication, deceptive presentation, unsupported claims, unlawful discrimination, undisclosed propaganda, or unsafe use.

Commercial quality review is not evidence that a deliverable is independent public-interest research. If a commercial deliverable is later adapted for public-interest publication, it must be reclassified as a new PI or PS workstream, independently validated, stripped of unauthorized confidential material, and accompanied by a provenance disclosure.

## 6. Financial and administrative separation

FWI shall maintain distinct project identifiers:

- `PI-YYYY-NNN` for independently initiated public-interest work;
- `PS-YYYY-NNN` for partner-supported public-interest work; and
- `CS-YYYY-NNN` for commercial services.

Income, direct costs, shared-cost allocations, in-kind support, reimbursements, and material staff time must be attributable to the relevant workstream. Commercial invoices and client payment records must not be represented as donations or grants. FWI must not imply that a payment is tax-deductible or charitable unless that claim is legally established for the relevant transaction and jurisdiction.

Commercial surplus may support general public-interest operations. Such support does not give any client influence over specific public-interest work and must be reported at an appropriate aggregate level when FWI publishes funding information. Restricted funding may be used only for its documented purpose.

## 7. Contract and acceptance controls

Before accepting external money or material in-kind support, the responsible human must document:

- the workstream classification and project identifier;
- payer, beneficial client, sponsor, donor, or partner identity;
- scope, deliverables, intended use, users, and distribution rights;
- funding amount or range and material in-kind support;
- editorial, factual-review, confidentiality, intellectual-property, correction, and termination terms;
- data provenance, access, retention, reuse, and deletion conditions;
- conflicts of interest and required recusals;
- human-rights, safety, sanctions, fraud, and reputation risks proportionate to the engagement; and
- accountable commercial, editorial, validation, data, and approval roles.

FWI shall decline or terminate an arrangement that requires concealed sponsorship, predetermined findings presented as independent, fabricated evidence, misleading endorsements, unlawful activity, abusive surveillance or profiling, improper use of confidential information, retaliation, or obstruction of a necessary correction.

## 8. Public labels and disclosures

Classification labels must appear prominently near the title or opening of a material publication and in promotional content where omission could mislead a reasonable person. A disclosure hidden only in an About page, footer, metadata field, or generic policy link is insufficient.

### 8.1 PI disclosure

> **FWI Public-Interest Research.** Independently initiated and editorially controlled by FutureWorld Intelligence. No external party purchased or controlled the findings.

If there is no external funding, the short label alone may be used. Material author or institutional conflicts must still be disclosed.

### 8.2 PS disclosure

> **Partner-Supported Public-Interest Research.** Supported by [name and form of support]. FutureWorld Intelligence retained control of methods, findings, validation, corrections, and publication. The supporter [describe any data, factual-review, or other role].

### 8.3 CS disclosure for public outputs

> **Commercial / Commissioned Work.** Prepared by FutureWorld Intelligence for [client] under a paid engagement. The client [describe scope, data, review, and publication role]. This work is not independently initiated FWI public-interest research.

If naming a client is lawfully restricted, the output may be public only when the omission itself would not be misleading; it must state the client category, the reason for non-identification, the nature of payment, and all material influence. The Institutional Lead and Editorial Reviewer must approve that exception.

## 9. Information, data, and technology separation

PI, PS, and CS records must use distinct project roots and access groups. Commercial contracts, client data, credentials, private communications, unpublished advice, and payment information must never be stored in the public website repository.

FWI shall apply least-privilege access, appropriate retention and deletion rules, and an auditable transfer process. Confidential commercial data may not be used in public-interest research, model training, demonstrations, marketing, or another client engagement without a valid lawful basis, necessary permission, and documented review. Public audience or analytics data may not be provided to clients for individual commercial, political, or manipulative profiling.

When a person works across classifications, access is granted by project need rather than institutional seniority. Shared tools may be used only if project separation, permissions, and confidentiality can be maintained.

## 10. People, conflicts, and decision rights

The following responsibilities must be assigned:

| Responsibility | Decision right |
|---|---|
| Institutional Lead | Approves classification, policy exceptions, and serious incident response |
| Editorial Authority | Controls PI/PS editorial decisions and public disclosures |
| Commercial Lead | Negotiates CS scope, budget, schedule, and client administration without controlling PI/PS findings |
| Validation Reviewer | Tests evidence and required records independently of commercial acceptance |
| Data Custodian | Approves access, transfer, retention, reuse, and deletion controls |
| Authorized Human Approver | Records the final release decision for the relevant classification |

People may hold multiple roles while FWI is small, but conflicts must be declared and mitigated. For PS work and any public CS output, the person who negotiated or accepted the payment may not be the sole classifier, sole editorial reviewer, and sole publication approver. A qualified external reviewer may provide the required independent check.

## 11. Brand, promotion, and endorsements

The FWI name and logo may identify both public-interest and commercial work only when the classification label is equally clear. Commercial clients may not imply that purchasing a service secures FWI institutional endorsement, favorable coverage, access to unpublished public-interest findings, or influence over future research.

Testimonials, endorsements, case studies, and partner logos require authorization and must disclose material relationships. Sponsored placement must be visually distinguishable from editorial content. Search results, social posts, thumbnails, video descriptions, and downloadable files must preserve the relevant classification where omission could mislead.

## 12. Publication, correction, and complaints

PI and PS work must follow the full FWI editorial and validation process. Public CS work must meet the same truthfulness, evidence, safety, copyright, AI, correction, and human-approval duties, even where its client-specific methodology differs.

A client or sponsor may request a correction but may not veto a necessary correction or require silent material alteration. Material commercial influence, misclassification, or missing disclosure discovered after publication requires prompt correction, relabelling, suspension, or withdrawal and a preserved audit record.

Concerns may be submitted to **futureworldintelligence@gmail.com**. Good-faith concerns about funding, influence, classification, or undisclosed commercial relationships must be reviewed without retaliation.

## 13. Baseline, legacy work, and transition

The public repository audit recorded in [Separation Implementation Record](SEPARATION_IMPLEMENTATION_RECORD.md) found no current commercial offer, pricing page, client portal, donation mechanism, commissioned-work catalogue, or sponsored-content label. The existing public website is therefore designated as FWI's public-interest publication channel from the effective date of this Policy.

This channel designation does not retroactively validate the accuracy or publication readiness of every item. Existing and future formal reports remain subject to FWI's report-level validation programme.

Any commercial engagement accepted after the effective date must use the classification record and separation controls before work begins. Legacy external relationships, if any exist outside the audited repository, must be classified before further work, payment, renewal, promotion, or publication.

## 14. Monitoring, exceptions, and enforcement

FWI shall review classifications, disclosures, access groups, and financial attribution at least annually and sample them during governance audits. A change in funder, client, scope, access, use, release plan, or editorial condition requires reclassification review.

Exceptions must be narrow, lawful, time-limited, documented, and approved by the Institutional Lead and Editorial Authority. No exception may permit concealed payment, purchased conclusions, fabricated evidence, deceptive classification, or unauthorized use of confidential data.

Material non-compliance may result in a publication hold, access removal, recusal, repayment or termination where appropriate, correction, public disclosure, withdrawal, process change, or termination of the relationship.

## 15. Reference framework

This Policy draws on transparency, independence, conflict-management, and remedy principles in:

- [UNESCO Media Development Indicators](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000163102)
- [UNESCO guidance on funding journalism without compromising editorial independence](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000381146)
- [OECD conflict-of-interest guidance](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/conflict-of-interest.html)
- [FTC guidance on clear disclosure of commercially sponsored or native content](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/native-advertising-guide-businesses)
- [UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/publications/guidingprinciplesbusinesshr_en.pdf)

These are alignment references, not claims of endorsement, jurisdiction-wide legal compliance, accreditation, or certification.

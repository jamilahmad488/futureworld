Climate Project Readiness

Nine questions to test before approaching a donor

01ProblemIs the climate or environmental problem specific, evidenced and geographically defined?
02PeopleAre affected and vulnerable groups identified with meaningful participation?
03BaselineCan current conditions be measured or credibly described?
04LogicDoes each activity plausibly lead to a measurable output and outcome?
05AdditionalityIs it clear what donor support enables that would not otherwise occur?
06SafeguardsAre environmental, social, gender and community risks addressed?
07CapacityCan the team demonstrate governance, implementation and financial controls?
08SustainabilityWill institutions, benefits and maintenance continue after grant closure?
09FitDoes the project meet the exact eligibility and priorities of the funder?

Questions Organizations Ask

Climate proposal and project-readiness FAQ

What makes a climate project grant-ready?

A grant-ready climate project clearly defines the climate problem, affected population, evidence baseline, intervention logic, measurable results, safeguards, implementation capacity, budget and sustainability pathway.

Can FWI guarantee that a donor will fund a proposal?

No. FWI improves readiness, evidence, structure and donor alignment but cannot guarantee funding or influence a donor’s independent decision.

Which types of climate projects can FWI support?

Lawful public-benefit initiatives involving community climate action, forestry, Assisted Natural Regeneration, watershed restoration, biodiversity, nurseries, climate resilience, monitoring and related livelihoods may be considered.

Can an early project idea be reviewed?

Yes. A short description can be assessed before a full proposal is prepared. Early diagnosis often prevents time being spent on weak intervention logic or an unsuitable funding opportunity.

Does FWI submit applications or receive grants for clients?

The precise role is agreed for each assignment. FWI provides advisory and writing support; the eligible applicant remains responsible for institutional representations, approvals, evidence and submission.
